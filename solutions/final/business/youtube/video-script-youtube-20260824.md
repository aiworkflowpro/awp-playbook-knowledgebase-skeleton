# The AI Editing Workflow I Actually Use

> Voiceover script for one YouTube video. Produced by `workflows/awp-video-script-workflow.md`, run on 2026-08-24. Target duration 6:00, spoken word count 801, which is about 134 words per minute. Research base: `research/topics/ai-video-editing/materials/202608/`.

## Hook (0:00–0:20)

Editing is the part of video work I am worst at. It used to take longer than filming and writing combined. So I built an AI editing workflow. By the end you will know my four steps, the two that pay back immediately, and where it still breaks.

## Value

### The problem (0:20–0:50)

Most editing advice assumes you enjoy editing. I do not. I came from software engineering into product management, so I think in steps, not in timelines and keyframes. Every tutorial I found showed a different tool. None of them showed an order to run those tools in. So I stopped collecting tools and wrote down a sequence instead. That sequence is what I am giving you.

### Step 1 — Organize the media before you cut (0:50–1:40)

The first step has no AI in it at all. Before anything else, I sort the footage, graphics, audio, and drafts into four folders. Every file gets a name with a date and a topic in it. This sounds trivial. It is the single change that saved me the most time. A tidy project edits faster and with fewer mistakes. When files are named at random, you cannot find anything after a month. Search stops working. Then, before I cut anything, I write the structure down — the sections, and how long each one runs. Planning first means I choose clips against a plan, instead of scrolling a timeline hoping something works.

### Step 2 — Let AI make the first pass (1:40–2:40)

Here is the rule I follow. AI is a first draft, never the final decision. AI handles the boring passes. Scene splits. Rough color. Subtitles. Clean audio. I handle the judgment. What stays, what goes, and what the edit feels like. That one line changed how I work. I stopped asking the tool to be good, and started asking it to be fast. Then I audit what it produced. Always audit. AI output that nobody checked is not a first draft. It is a guess. In practice the audit is simple. The tool proposes a cut list, and I play a few seconds on either side of every cut. That pass takes about ten minutes. It is the one part of editing I refuse to hand over, because a wrong cut is the mistake viewers always notice.

### Step 3 — Subtitles and clean audio first (2:40–3:40)

If you take one thing from this video, take this step. Of everything AI does in editing, two jobs pay back immediately. Subtitles and noise removal. Both are one click in most tools now. Subtitles matter because many viewers watch with the sound off, and because YouTube searches your captions. Clean audio matters because bad sound makes people leave faster than a bad picture does. Let me put a number on it. Cleaning audio by hand used to take me half an hour per video, and I still got it wrong. Captions took longer, because I typed them. Now both run while I make coffee. The result is not perfect. I still fix names and technical terms the transcriber mangles, and that correction takes about five minutes.

### Step 4 — Edit the text, not the timeline (3:40–4:40)

This is the step that felt strange at first. You transcribe the video, then you edit by deleting words from the transcript. The video follows the text. For a tutorial like this one, that is the biggest shift in editing I have seen. I write in text. I think in text. Now I cut in text as well. A rough cut that used to take a whole evening now takes about forty minutes. There is a catch, and nobody mentions it. This works when someone is speaking on camera, and it does nothing for anything else. If the video is mostly screen recording, the transcript cannot tell you what is on screen. So I cut the voice track in text, and cut the visuals the old way, against the plan from step one.

### Where it breaks (4:40–5:20)

Now the honest part. This workflow does not make a video look polished. It removes the boring work. It does not add motion graphics, animation, or design. That is still my weak spot, and no tool has fixed it for me yet. I am learning that part slowly, and I would rather say so than pretend. AI also gets scene splits wrong on slow footage, so I check every cut it proposes. If you came here for a video that looks like a studio made it, this workflow will not get you there.

## Recap (5:20–5:45)

So, four steps. Organize the media and plan the structure. Let AI take the first pass, and keep the judgment. Run subtitles and clean audio before anything else. Then edit the transcript instead of the timeline. If you only do one this week, do the third one. It takes two minutes and changes how finished the video feels.
